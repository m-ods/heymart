# Wikipedia Manual of Style - Reference for Heypedia

Condensed guidelines from [Wikipedia:Manual of Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style) and [MoS/Biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Biography), applied to this project.

## General tone

- Write in straightforward, succinct, and easily understood language.
- Maintain a neutral point of view (NPOV). Do not use promotional or subjective language.
- Let facts speak for themselves. Write clinically.
- Avoid overuse of emphasis (italics, bold). Prefer rewriting for clarity.
- Do not use first-person ("I", "we") or second-person ("you").
- Refer to the subject by last name after the first full-name mention (e.g., "Schweiger" after introducing "Martin Schweiger").

## Lead section (biographical)

- Summarise the life and works with due weight.
- The first sentence should state:
  1. Full name (bold), with birth name if different
  2. Birth date in parentheses: `(born 19 January 2001)`
  3. Nationality/context for notability
  4. One or more noteworthy roles the person is mainly known for
- Do not overload the first sentence. Spread information across the lead paragraph.
- Ethnicity, religion, or sexuality: only in the lead if relevant to notability.
- Birth/death places belong in the body, not the opening date brackets.

### First sentence pattern

> **Full Name** (born DD Month YYYY) is a [nationality] [role/occupation] known for [reason for notability].

## Section organization

1. Lead section (no heading, concise summary)
2. Body sections with `==` headings
3. Appendices in order: See also, References, Further reading, External links
4. Categories last

## Section headings

- Use sentence case: "Early life and education", not "Early Life And Education"
- Do not redundantly refer to the subject: "Early life", not "Schweiger's early life"
- Do not phrase as questions
- Do not number or letter headings
- Headings must be unique within the page

## Formatting

- **Bold**: Subject's name at first mention in the lead only.
- *Italics*: Titles of works (books, films, games, albums). Not for quotations.
- Links: Use descriptive anchor text. Link to Wikipedia for well-known concepts on first mention.
- Dates: Use a consistent format throughout. Either "19 January 2001" (DMY) or "January 19, 2001" (MDY). Do not mix.
- Abbreviations: Write out in full on first occurrence, abbreviation in parentheses.

## Biographical specifics

- Opening paragraph provides context: nationality, what made them notable.
- Positions/roles: Emphasise what made the person notable. Avoid listing incidental roles.
- Avoid defining people by relationships (spouse of, child of) unless contextually relevant.
- Criminal acts: describe the specific act, not labels.
- Names: Use the most recognizable form. Full name at article start.

## Quotations

- Brief quotations to illustrate a point or attribute a view. Never excessive.
- Do not italicise quotations. Quotation marks alone suffice.
- Must be accompanied by inline citation from reliable source.
- Retain original wording faithfully (principle of minimal change).

## References

- Direct quotations must have inline citations.
- Superscript reference numbers link to footnotes.
- References section uses ordered list.

## Common pitfalls to avoid

- Promotional or puffery language ("renowned", "legendary", "iconic")
- Peacock terms ("one of the best", "widely considered")
- Weasel words ("some say", "it is believed", "many people think")
- Editorialising or inserting opinion as fact
- Redundant links (don't link the same term twice)
- Over-capitalisation (sentence case for headings and most terms)
